Home in landscape

Design preview S1 for the Deliverance launcher. Screen: Galaxy S20 FE turned on its side, drawn at 844 x 390 (the phone itself is 914 x 411 dp; everything scales to fit). Every look piece is the approved C5 design. Nothing here is built yet.

Where the screen is spent

Landscape gives you a wide but short screen (390 high instead of 844). Three things take space away from the sides, and the design keeps clear of all three:

  1. The camera hole moves to a short edge: the left edge when the top of the phone points left, the right edge when it points right. It sits halfway down that edge.
  2. The navigation buttons (you use the 3-button bar) sit on the right side, about 48 wide. Android may put them on the left when the phone is turned the other way (UNVERIFIED on your phone); the layout reads the real sides, so it works either way.
  3. The status bar stays hidden on Home, as in portrait. The pill takes its job.

Usable content area: 760 x 374 (dashed gold line in the pictures). The code draws it on this stage and scales it up to the real free area, as it does today for portrait.

Layout A: three columns, everything visible, no scrolling

Left column: header, Treasury, pennantsMiddle: orb, shields, dockRight: Ledger, command bar, DispatchesPhone turned so the camera is on the LEFT (nav buttons on the right)
  1. Header moves to the top of the left column. Date row on top, clock and weather under it on one line. Same weekday medallion and divider.
  2. Treasury and the four pennants sit under it, narrower. The Treasury quest bar drops under the total.
  3. Orb stays in the exact middle of the screen, ring text and glow unchanged. Two shields each side, as in portrait.
  4. Dock becomes a row of four wax seals under the orb, the same size as in portrait.
  5. Ledger, command bar, Dispatches stack in the right column, narrower.

Good: all sections are on screen at once, nothing to scroll, closest to the portrait feel. Cost: six sections must be re-flowed to a narrower width (the code today fixes them at 361 wide). Smallest text stays at 9 to 10 pt after scaling.

Layout B: Oracle on the left, one scrolling column on the right

Left: orb, shields and dock exactly as in portraitRight: portrait column, scrolls, command bar pinnedPhone turned so the camera is on the RIGHT (nav buttons on the left)
  1. Oracle half (orb, four shields, label, dock) is the portrait picture unchanged, centred in the left half. The orb is a little larger.
  2. Right half is a portrait-width column: header, Treasury, pennants, Ledger fit on the first view. Dispatches is below the fold: slide the column up to read it.
  3. Command bar is pinned at the bottom of the right column so it is always one tap away.

Good: the sections keep their portrait size and look (cheapest to build, least risk). Cost: Dispatches is not visible without a scroll, and a scrolling column fights Home's own up/down swipes (the code must hand over only at the ends).

Camera hole and pill

The pill is drawn around the hole, so in landscape it must follow the hole to the edge. Options:

P1 Standing pill (recommended)

A black capsule stands on the edge around the hole, same gold hairline. Waiting-notification icons stack above the hole (max 4, then +n), Hushed, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and signal stack below. Nothing else on Home goes within 36 of that edge, so nothing sits under it.

P2 Hide the pill

The same icons move into a thin strip at the top of the right column (rotated phone has no room for a capsule). Simpler, but the hole is then just a black dot with no frame.

Dock, shields, tap reactions

  • Shields and seals keep their portrait size (tap targets at least 46 dp). The press flash and the 120 ms delay before the app starts are unchanged.
  • The dock never touches the navigation bar: it stays at least 12 dp away from it, and a thin gold line separates the two.
  • Orb tap, orb glow, ring light, wake and charging reactions are unchanged. Nothing new animates all the time.

Command bar, typing layer and keyboard

  1. Tap the command bar: the typing layer opens over Home, as in portrait. The text field stays on top and the keyboard comes up from below; the screen above the keyboard is only about 200 high.
  2. So in landscape the layer has two panes: most-used apps (4 x 2) on the left, the framed panel with starred contacts and quick chips on the right. Search results use the same two panes.
  3. Typed text stays when you rotate with the keyboard open (the screen is not rebuilt).
  4. The home gesture / Home button still closes the layer.

The transition

Press the buttons to watch it (the real thing is on the phone; this is a model with simple blocks, drawn at half size, using layout A):

What you see (about 350 ms, one time)

  1. You turn the phone. Android turns the screen; the launcher does not restart.
  2. Each section glides from its portrait spot to its landscape spot and changes size on the way, using a soft ease in and out (350 ms). The orb barely moves: it is centred in both.
  3. Text and glow keep drawing while the sections move; pressing something mid-way still works.
  4. Animations setting Off, or Android's remove-animations, or Battery Saver: the sections jump to the new spot at once, no motion.

What the code must do

  1. Tell Android the launcher handles rotation itself (manifest configChanges for orientation and screen size on the launcher activity). Without it the whole activity is destroyed and rebuilt on every turn: flicker, lost typed text, the layer closing. This is one line in an upstream file, in its own commit.
  2. Ask Android for a quick cross-fade instead of its slow turning picture (rotationAnimation on the window). UNVERIFIED on One UI 5: if Samsung ignores it, the system animation plays under ours, which is still fine.
  3. One animated number (0 = portrait, 1 = landscape) drives every section's position and size. It starts only when the orientation changes and is not run on the first draw, so launching the launcher is as quick as now.
  4. The launch delay (120 ms), press flashes and all reactions are untouched; they live inside each section.

What changes versus portrait, numbered

  1. The stage: 390 x 842 portrait becomes 760 x 374 in landscape, and is scaled by the free width and height (minus nav bar and camera strip on their real sides).
  2. Sections no longer share one column; each has a landscape home (layout A or B).
  3. Six sections (header, Treasury, pennants, Ledger, command bar, Dispatches) take a width instead of the fixed 361 (layout A only).
  4. The dock goes from a row at the bottom of everything to a row under the orb (A) or the Oracle half (B).
  5. The pill stands up along the hole's edge (P1).
  6. The typing layer gets two panes and respects the camera and nav-bar sides.
  7. Rotation: no activity restart; a 350 ms glide; the turn is allowed only if the phone's own Auto-rotate is on and the launcher's rotation lock is off.

Is it feasible? What I found in the code

ItemFinding
Rotation lockSharedLauncherActivity.kt: when the launcher setting "fixed rotation" (Settings, Home screen) is on, the screen is forced to portrait; when off, it follows the phone's own Auto-rotate. Default is off. So landscape can already be reached; today it would show a tiny portrait strip. Note: the upstream "Landscape" choice also forces portrait; harmless because the app only offers on/off.
ManifestNo configChanges on LauncherActivity: Android rebuilds the whole activity on rotation. Our typing layer state (open or closed) is a plain remember, so it would be lost. Fix = declare the changes (planned).
Stage scaleMedievalHomeComponent.kt: one 390 x 842 stage scaled by min(width/390, height/842), minus bottom nav bar and top bar. In landscape that gives scale 0.43 (a narrow strip). Needs a second stage and the real left/right bars.
Camera pillMedievalPill.kt looks for a cutout at the top edge; on a side edge it finds none and falls back to the top centre (wrong). Needs a side-hole finder and a vertical capsule layout.
InsetsOnly bottom nav and top status inset are read; left/right bars and the side camera cutout are not. Typing layer: same (top and bottom only).
SectionsEach is drawn at fixed coordinates in a 361-wide body (A needs a width parameter for six of them; B can place them as they are).
Motion gateThe glide uses the existing gate in MedievalMotion.kt (Full/Minimal animate, Off snaps). No always-running animation is added.
Not verifiable hereHow Samsung rotates (does it honour a cross-fade, which side the nav bar takes, whether Home can rotate with its own switch). All on the phone.
